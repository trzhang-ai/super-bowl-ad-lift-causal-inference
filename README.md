# Measuring the Search Lift from NerdWallet's 2025 Super Bowl Ad

How much did NerdWallet's Super Bowl LIX ad raise brand search interest, and for how long? A Super Bowl spot is one of the most expensive media buys available, and the brand-demand lift is the starting point for valuing it. The ad aired nationally, so there was no holdout group. I used public [Google Trends data](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=2024-11-01%202025-02-28&geo=US&q=%2Fm%2F0hzpb7j,credit%20card%20points,travel%20credit%20card,best%20savings%20account&hl=en) to estimate what NerdWallet searches would have been without the ad, and measured the gap.

![NerdWallet search interest vs. counterfactual](figures/lift_vs_counterfactual.png)

## Key takeaways

- **The ad produced a large, immediate lift.** On 10 February, NerdWallet search interest was **63 index points above** the no-ad estimate, about **2.7×** the expected level.
- **The lift was short-lived.** It averaged **+23 points per day over days 0–3** (95% interval: 17 to 36). About two-thirds of it came on day 0, and search interest was back near baseline by day 4.
- **What it means for planning.** For future tentpole campaigns, paid-search budgets, landing pages and site capacity should be sized for day 0. Retargeting and follow-up have a window of days, not weeks.

## Approach

The no-ad estimate is built from three personal-finance searches that follow the same seasonal pattern as NerdWallet but had no direct reason to respond to its ad: *credit card points*, *travel credit card* and *best savings account*.

I compared four causal inference methods and chose between them with an out-of-sample test. Each model was re-run on 38 dates before the ad, when the true effect is zero, and the model with the smallest false effects was preferred.

| Method | Average lift, days 0–3 |
|---|---|
| **Synthetic control (preferred)** | **23.3** |
| Difference-in-differences | 21.9 |
| Event study | 21.6 |
| Interrupted time series | 13–17 |

## Why the estimate is credible

- **It tracks NerdWallet before the ad.** The synthetic NerdWallet follows the real one through the holiday season and the turn-of-year rise, with no unusual movement in the week before the broadcast.
- **It finds nothing when there is nothing to find.** On the 38 dates without an ad, no run produced an effect close to the real one (largest 13.3 against 23.3; p = 0.026), and the false effects averaged close to zero (+0.7).
- **It holds when the choices change.** The estimate stays between 22.7 and 24.5 for every pre-period length from 30 to 100 days. Leaving out one control search moves it the most, to between 19.1 and 26.9, and it stays clearly positive in every case.

## Caveats

- Google Trends is a relative, sampled index, not search volume, so the estimates are in index points for this data export.
- The method assumes the ad did not move the control searches, which cannot be tested. The controls dip slightly on day 0, so the day-0 lift may be overstated: against NerdWallet's own 30-day average it is 48.5 points rather than 62.7.

## Next steps

- Translate the index lift into business value by mapping it to absolute search volume, site sessions and sign-ups.
- Measure future campaigns with a geo-holdout design, which estimates the lift experimentally.
